# ADR-008: A background upload session, and an idempotency key for the clip

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-11-16  
**Scope:** `POST /v1/videos` (the `Idempotency-Key` header, `feed_videos.upload_key`, migration 023), `BackgroundClipUploader`, `SystemBackgroundUploadSession`, `UploadRecordStore`, `UploadBodyStager`, `IdempotentReplayResolver`, `ClipUploading` and its use cases, the Upload tab, sign-out and sign-in

## Context

A video upload was one foreground `URLSession` request. iOS suspends an app a few seconds after it leaves the screen, so a viewer who switched apps or locked the phone met an error and started again from the first byte, and starting again could leave a second clip, because the Worker could not tell a repeated upload from a new one. The first cut of this work (streaming the clip from disk, a progress bar and a 30-second `beginBackgroundTask`) saves only a clip that finishes inside that grace period.

This cut has four goals: the clip arrives although the viewer leaves the app, locks the phone, is offline for a while or the system ends the app; a retry never makes a second clip; the Upload tab always ends in a state the viewer can act on (done, or failed with **Try again**), never a spinner that does not end; and the avatar upload, the dubbed-audio upload and every installed build keep working unchanged.

**What was measured** (`docs/superpowers/notes/background-upload-spike.md`, cited by its ids: Part A on the simulator, Part B on an iPhone on iOS 27.2.1 against the production Worker):

- **D1: a task created while the app is in the foreground starts at once.** Its request began within 0.11 s of `resume()` on Wi-Fi and within 0.6 s under a 3G profile (B1 to B5). One Apple forum report describes background uploads deferred for hours under an energy or data budget (many files, 20 GB and more); a single clip was not deferred.
- **D2: a task created in a background wake does not start until the app is active.** It began 223 s after `resume()`, 0.75 s after the app became active (B5, one run; the cause is not established). So the app resubmits nothing in the background: `UploadPolicy.resubmitsWhileInBackground` is `false` in production, a 401 met in a background wake ends as `failed(interrupted)`, and the viewer's Try again at the next open sends it again.
- **D3: the system relaunches the app through the scene's `.backgroundTask(.urlSession(id))`, with no app delegate.** After the process was ended with `devicectl` (a SIGKILL, which the system does not treat as a swipe-away) the upload went on and the system relaunched the app in the background within 0.11 s of the answer. The recreated session received its events first, and the scene closure ran 0.10 to 0.24 s after them (B6, B5 leg 1). The staged body kept the default file protection and the record files were readable at the relaunch.
- **A rejected token costs one whole upload.** The Worker answers a bad token with 401 before it reads the body, but nothing reached the app during the 346 s that a 12.4 MB body took to leave the phone on the 3G profile (36 KB/s); the 401 then arrived 1.25 s after the last byte as an ordinary completion, neither a hang nor an error (B8, R8).
- **`totalBytesSent` runs ahead of the wire.** It counts bytes handed to the socket: on the 3G profile it read 100% 52 s before the last byte left the phone (B8), and on the simulator three seconds before a slow receiver had read the body (A1b).
- **What reaches the delegate.** For an upload task `urlSession(_:dataTask:didReceive:completionHandler:)` is never called, so the status is read from the task when it ends. The Worker's answer body does reach `didReceive data`, also in a relaunched process (A1, A2, B7). `urlSessionDidFinishEvents` never arrives while the app is in the foreground (A1). After a relaunch the system still lists a finished task until its events are delivered (B6, B5), so the uploader never filters `getAllTasks` by state.
- **An answer before the body is read** (A4, the case of a stored key). On loopback a receiver that answered and drained the body behaved like a normal 200; one that answered and closed with the body unread was never reported by a background session in 125 s, while the system retried seven times. What the real Worker does is not known until migration 023 and the deploy.

## Decision

- **A background `URLSession` file upload.** The system's transfer daemon sends the staged body for the app, goes on when the app is suspended or ended, and relaunches the app to say how it ended. The session is `com.somsak.Marauders.upload`: `isDiscretionary = false` (the viewer asked for the upload), cellular, expensive and constrained networks allowed, `timeoutIntervalForResource` of 24 hours (the ceiling for one upload, so a task that cannot finish ends as a failure and the tab never waits for ever), `sessionSendsLaunchEvents = true`. There is one instance per process, made in `MaraudersApp.init` before any view, so a relaunch finds it by its identifier. No `UIBackgroundModes` and no `Info.plist` change are needed (D3 relaunched the app without one). A task is `uploadTask(with:fromFile:)`, the only upload form a background session accepts, and its `taskDescription` is the record id.
- **The body is staged in a durable file, with a record beside it.** Tapping Upload writes the multipart body (the chunked `MultipartBodyWriter` of the first cut) to `Application Support/PendingUpload/<id>.multipart` and a record to `<id>.json`. The folder is excluded from backup and its files keep the default file protection, never `.complete`, because a locked phone must still be able to read the body. `UploadRecord` (version 1) holds the id, the account's `sub`, the body file name, the boundary, the byte count, the creation time and the state, and nothing else: no token, caption, coordinates or clip path (a finished record also holds the clip the Worker answered with, until the viewer has seen it). There is one upload at a time. Fewer than the clip's size plus 50 MiB free fails before anything is written; that check reads `volumeAvailableCapacityForImportantUsage`, a required-reason API, so `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` declares `NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryDiskSpace` with reason `E174.1`, without which App Store Connect refuses the build. The export in `tmp` is deleted once the body is staged.
- **The record's id is the idempotency key.** It is sent as `Idempotency-Key` (lower-case) with every submission of the body, Try again included. `POST /v1/videos` reads the header before it reads the body: a UUID, lower-cased; anything else, the empty string included, is **400** `Idempotency-Key must be a UUID` (never "no header", which would lose the protection silently). A key this account already stored is answered **200** with the stored clip, with no body read, no R2 write and no insert, so a replay needs no multipart body. A new key is stored with the clip (`feed_videos.upload_key`, migration 023, unique per `(owner_user_id, upload_key)` through a partial index) and answered **201**, as without a key. Two requests with one key leave one clip: the loser deletes the object it wrote and answers 200 with the winner's clip. The key is scoped to the account that sent it, a replay counts against the `upload` rate limit, and a request without the header takes today's path with today's SQL, so every installed build keeps working. The key is a header, not a form field, so the Worker can answer before it reads up to 100 MB.
- **An actor owns the record's life.** `BackgroundClipUploader` conforms to the Domain protocol `ClipUploading` (`start`, `snapshots`, `retry`, `discard`, `reconcile`, reached through `ClipUploadUseCases`) over a seam, `BackgroundUploadSession`, which `SystemBackgroundUploadSession` implements; tests drive the actor through a fake of the seam. The session's delegate calls become an ordered stream of events that the actor applies one at a time. After every `await` in a change of state it reads the record again and stops when the record is gone or no longer in the state it expected, so a late or reentrant event changes nothing and recreates nothing. It publishes `UploadSnapshot`s (`idle`, `uploading(fraction)`, `finishing`, `finished(clip)`, `failed(message, canRetry)`), `UploadVideoViewModel` only watches them, and a finished clip is reported to the feed once, when the app is active and a viewer is signed in (a finished record stays on disk while nobody is, for the next sign in to keep or delete). It never signs anyone out and never clears a store: a token the Worker rejects for good is reported through a reporter that the root view connects once, for the container it keeps (`AuthViewModel.handleRejectedAccessToken`, as for every other client), and it works with none. It never needs the container or a view, so a launch by the system can finish an upload alone.
- **A lost answer is asked for, not sent again.** For a 2xx whose answer never arrived whole, `IdempotentReplayResolver` sends the upload's own request with no body, which the Worker answers with the stored clip; if that fails the upload ends as `failed(responseLost)`. A 401 is healed once, with the shared `TokenRefresher` (never a second refresher: a refresh token is single use, [ADR-007](007-token-refresh.md)) and the same body and key sent again, only while the app is active (D2). Every other failure (429, 502, 503, 504, a 5xx, a transport failure) is a failure with **Try again**, and the app never sends the body again by itself after one. A 4xx other than 401 and 429 can never succeed with the same body, so the body is deleted and only **Choose another video** is offered.
- **The stored upload is checked at every launch and sign in** (`reconcile`, never on a return to the foreground). A body that no record owns, a record 7 days after its stored `createdAt` (never read from a file date: file-timestamp APIs are privacy-manifest APIs) and, when a token is stored, a record of another account (an unreadable `sub` counts as another account) are deleted; an `uploading` record whose task is gone becomes `failed(interrupted)`. A sign out discards the pending upload (task, record, body) before the session is cleared; both hooks are bounded to 3 s, so a stuck uploader never holds the viewer back. When the system relaunches the app for the session's events, the scene's `.backgroundTask(.urlSession(id))` waits (25 s at most, and not at all when the events were already applied, as in both relaunches measured) until the actor has applied them.
- **A bar never says 100% before the Worker has answered.** `UploadSnapshot.uploading(fraction:)` stays below 1; every byte sent is `finishing`, shown as `Finishing…`, because `totalBytesSent` runs ahead of the wire. Progress is saved and published only when the whole percent changes.

## Alternatives

- **A. `beginBackgroundTask` alone** (the 30-second safety net of the first cut). Small, no Worker change, testable on the simulator, but it saves only a clip that finishes inside the grace period iOS gives (about 30 s, not guaranteed). Kept: `UploadVideoViewModel` still holds one around the hand-over, which copies the clip into the body file.
- **B. `BGContinuedProcessingTask` (iOS 26 and later).** Not chosen. The Apple Developer Forums document a bug in which the task pauses or expires when the screen is locked (Apple's DTS engineer called it a bug and expected a fix in iOS 26.1; a developer still reported it on iOS 26.1 and 26.2 beta 3 with an S3 upload, and nothing was found for iOS 27). A cancel by the viewer cannot be told from an expiry by the system, on some devices the task never starts, and it can only be proven on a phone. Locking the phone is one of the commonest ways to leave an app.
- **D. A resumable or multipart upload through R2.** The most robust, and the only one that also removes the Worker's memory ceiling, but it rewrites the Worker and the client. Not now.
- **Resubmitting from a background wake** after a healed 401, so that an expired token never shows as a failure. Rejected on the evidence (D2): the task would not start until the app is active, which is when Try again would send it anyway.
- **A replay before every Try again, and a watchdog for an answer that comes before the body is read.** Rejected on the evidence (B8, R8): the Worker's early 401 was an ordinary completion, neither a hang nor an error. The one early answer not seen yet is the Worker's reply to a stored key (A4); the device check covers it.

## Consequences

- **Deploy order:** migration 023, then the Worker, then the app (a TestFlight build is the owner's Build Now; a push builds nothing). An app that sends the key to a Worker that has not run the migration is not supported: a Worker with the key code and no column answers **500** `upload lookup failed` to every keyed request, and an older Worker ignores the header, so a repeat is not recognised. The Worker change is compatible with every installed build, which send no key. A rolled-back Worker leaves the column harmless; `ALTER TABLE feed_videos DROP COLUMN upload_key` removes it and its index.
- **A swipe-away ends the upload.** iOS cancels an app's background transfers when the viewer swipes the app away from the app switcher, and nothing prevents it. The next launch shows `The upload was interrupted. Try again.`, and the record and its body stay for Try again (the same body under the same key). The spike did not measure it: `devicectl` ends the process with a SIGKILL, which the system did not treat as a swipe-away (B6, B5); the device check does.
- **The export still needs the app on screen.** Leaving the app during "Preparing video…" (the 720p export, before Upload is tapped) is not covered: `UploadVideoViewModel.prepare` holds no background task, and the session takes over only at the hand-over. The design lists it as a non-goal.
- **A token refresh inside a background wake can be cut off by suspension.** [ADR-007](007-token-refresh.md) lists a refresh in a background wake as not covered: the Worker would read the repeated token as reuse and end the family. The token is checked when Upload is tapped, in the foreground, and traded first when its `exp` has passed (`start` and Try again do both), so a 401 that reaches a background wake means a token the Worker rejects (revoked, rotated, signed out elsewhere), which is rare. `BackgroundLocationMonitor` accepts the same risk.
- **A rejected token costs a whole upload.** The 401 reaches the app only after the body has gone out (346 s for 12.4 MB on the 3G profile, B8), and the body goes out a second time after the refresh. The plan stands as written: no replay before Try again and no watchdog on the 401 and 429 rows.
- **A `staged` record left by a crash** (the process ended between saving the record and submitting the task) is sent by `reconcile` at the next launch with the stored token as it is. Unlike `start` and Try again it does not trade an expired token first, so the 401 then comes only after the whole body; and when that launch is a background relaunch, the task it creates waits for the foreground (D2).
- **The replay is unmeasured against the real Worker.** The Worker's reply to the replay with no body (A4) is not known until migration 023 and the deploy, and the device check covers it: the same body sent twice under one key must answer 201 and then 200 with the same clip. If the Worker closes the connection with the body unread (A4a), a background session reported nothing for the 125 s the spike waited, and a Try again on a stored key would show an upload that does not end (what finally ends it was not measured; the 24-hour resource timeout is the ceiling the design relies on). A replay can only find a clip stored by the account that sends it (the Worker scopes the key by owner), so one under another account misses and the upload ends as `failed(responseLost)`: `The clip may have been uploaded, but the answer was lost. Try again.`
- **The Worker's memory limit is unmeasured.** It buffers a body two or three times over (`formData`, `arrayBuffer`, the fast-start copy) in an isolate with 128 MB, so the client's 100 MB cap may be above what it can take. This change does not make that worse (the same bytes arrive), and a Try again on a clip the Worker cannot hold meets the same failure; approach D would remove the limit.
- **A lost answer after a commit is not handled.** If the insert commits but its answer never reaches the Worker, the existing rollback deletes the R2 object although the row exists, and a replay of that key then answers 200 with a clip whose file is gone (the viewer can delete it from Profile like any other). Before this change a retry made a second, healthy clip beside the broken one; now the retry returns the broken one. It needs a connection failure at the moment of the commit, and looking the row up before the rollback would handle it. Not built.
- **A session the Worker rejects** (a 401 that a refresh cannot heal) keeps the upload as a failure (`Your session ended. Sign in to try again.`), so the same account can finish after signing in again; a sign out discards it; a record of another account is deleted at launch and at sign in; and any record is deleted by the first launch or sign in after it is 7 days old. The unsent clip therefore stays in the app's own sandbox, on a phone that looks signed out, until that launch or sign in. A clip of such a session that finished is reported to the feed only once a viewer is signed in; when another account's sign-in check runs past its 3 s while that record is still on disk, the new account is shown it: an accepted, rare gap.
- **The old in-process video path stays in the code, unwired:** `UploadFeedVideoUseCase`, `VideoUploadRepository`, `DefaultVideoUploadRepository` and `VideoUploadAPIClient.upload`, with their tests. Nothing in Presentation or Composition names them, and deleting them is the owner's call. `VideoUploadForm` holds what they and the uploader share.
- **Under UI tests** the Upload tab gets an `IdleClipUploader`, which never uploads, and no UI test covers upload (a real one would reach the production Worker). `BackgroundClipUploader` is the seventh actor ([ADR-002](002-ios-concurrency.md)).
- **Not yet checked with the real app on a phone** (the device check, each upload a real clip to delete from Profile afterwards; of the cases below the spike's own build ran only the process ended with `devicectl` and relaunched, B6 and B5, and a locked phone inside it, B3): Home during an upload, a locked phone, Airplane mode on and off, ending the process with `devicectl` and relaunching, a swipe-away, a 401 healed through `DEBUG_INVALIDATE_ACCESS_TOKEN`, and the same body sent twice under one key.
